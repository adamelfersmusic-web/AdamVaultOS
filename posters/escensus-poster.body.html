The rookie gamble

Every time you hand a premium batch of leads to a new agent you can't see, you're running an expensive experiment — and you don't find out the result until the money's already gone.

You could catch exactly where they're losing the sale if you had time to listen to every call. You don't. So the audio sits in a folder, the rookie gives a vague excuse when the deal dies, and you end up paying for broad, generic training when what they needed was one specific fix.

It's not a training program.

04
The whole system

Two questions. Everything falls out of them.

1
Are your new agents following the framework?

We read for the meaning of each milestone. Full credit when an agent makes the move in their own words — flagged only when the move is actually missing.

2
Are they fixing what you tell them to fix?

One coaching priority a week. Not twelve — one. The next week we check whether it actually changed. That's the difference between training and development.

What you stop doing
  • Rookies telling long stories about calls they can't explain
  • Trainers burning hours hunting for the problem
  • Handing leads to agents who aren't ready
What you get instead
  • A weekly per-agent Ramp Report in plain English
  • A human ear on the 30 seconds that mattered
  • A monthly read: who's improving, who's stuck — by name
