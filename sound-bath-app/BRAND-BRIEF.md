# Brand brief — Bed

**Self-contained.** Paste the whole thing. It assumes no repo access and no
prior context, so it can go to any design tool or any human.

---

## The prompt

```
BRAND IDENTITY BRIEF — "Bed"

Design the visual identity for a product called Bed. Working name; you may
propose alternatives, but solve for this one first.

────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT IT IS

Bed is a tool for sound bath practitioners — the people who play crystal
singing bowls and gongs and lead group sound healing sessions. It generates the
sustained musical bed underneath a session: sub bass, drones, sparse motifs,
and nature texture. It also tells everyone in the room what to play and when.

It is not a listening app. Nobody puts on headphones and relaxes to it. It is
an INSTRUMENT operated by a person standing in front of a room, the way a
lighting console is operated.

────────────────────────────────────────
WHO IT IS FOR, AND THE TRANSFORMATION

A person who genuinely loves sound. They have collected bowls for years. They
have real presence and can hold a room. What they cannot do is compose, arrange,
or run a DAW — which is currently the thing standing between them and leading a
real session.

The promise is not "make better sound baths." It is:

    You already have the love, the instruments, and the presence.
    This is the first thing that lets you lead with them.

The hero of this brand is ALWAYS the person, never the software. The product's
job is to disappear. One line that captures it: "one violin player becomes a
symphony."

────────────────────────────────────────
THE ROOM IT LIVES IN — read this carefully, it is the whole design problem

These are real, ticketed events. 40 to 100 people lying on the floor under
blankets. Candles by the hundred. Sometimes projection on the ceiling, gongs on
frames, crystal bowls glowing on LED pads, live musicians improvising over the
top. Venues range from yoga studios to grand halls with chandeliers to
warehouses.

Which means:

- The room is DARK, and washed in warm amber from candlelight and tungsten.
- Screens are read at about four feet, at an oblique angle, in that low light.
- The person reading it has a mallet in each hand.
- A bright white screen in that room is genuinely disrespectful, and everyone
  present knows it. Any identity that only works on white is disqualified.
- Warm colours disappear into the ambient wash. Cool colours survive it.

This is not a phone-in-a-cafe product. Design for a dark room full of people
with their eyes closed.

────────────────────────────────────────
REGISTER

Modern and confident, but wellness. It has to be credible to two communities
that are suspicious of each other: people who think technology cheapens
something sacred, and people who don't take sound healing seriously at all.

Think closer to a beautifully made instrument or a piece of ceremonial equipment
than to software. Draw from yoga, tea ceremony, and ritual objects.

The tone is calm, precise, and quietly premium. This community buys $800 singing
bowls without blinking — they are not looking for cheap, and they are not
looking for clinical.

────────────────────────────────────────
HARD ANTI-REFERENCES — do not land on any of these

- SPA / WELLNESS CLICHÉ. No lotus flowers, no mandalas, no chakra rainbow, no
  soft-focus gradients, no watercolour blobs, no Zapfino-style scripts, no
  namaste-adjacent anything.
- MUSIC PRODUCTION SOFTWARE. No knobs, no faders, no waveforms, no keyboard
  graphics, no plugin-rack skeuomorphism, no "Aeonis Pad" fantasy naming.
- GENERIC AI DARK MODE. Not near-black plus one acid green or electric violet
  accent, rounded cards, gradient headings, glowing orbs.
- CRYSTAL / NEW AGE ECOMMERCE. No amethyst photography, no sacred geometry
  wallpaper, no gold foil on purple.
- MEDITATION APP. Not Calm, not Headspace. Those are for the person receiving.
  This is for the person leading.

────────────────────────────────────────
THINGS THAT ARE TRUE ABOUT THE PRODUCT, IF THEY HELP YOU

- It works by holding four layers: a sub you feel rather than hear, a sustained
  drone, sparse bell-like motifs, and nature texture. Four is a real number in
  this product, not a decoration.
- Its signature move: the whole room slowly changes key over thirty seconds
  without anyone noticing it happen.
- The practitioner's own phrase for what they do is "holding space."
- It never states a musical third — deliberately, so the humans in the room get
  to decide the emotion. The product's whole character is deference.
- The name "Bed" means three things at once: the musical bed, the thing people
  are lying on, and the sense of being held.

────────────────────────────────────────
DELIVERABLES

1. WORDMARK. Primary lockup plus a compact/icon form that reads at 20px and as
   a favicon.
2. COLOUR. A full palette with hex values, built to be legible on a dark
   surface in a warm-lit room. State clearly what each colour MEANS — one of
   them should signify "this is live and making sound," and it should be the
   only thing that ever uses it.
3. TYPE. A pairing — one voice for identity, one for interface. Interface type
   must stay legible at four feet in low light, so specify sizes and weights
   with that in mind, not desk viewing.
4. ONE SCREEN, mocked. The performance view: what section we're in, its
   intention, time remaining, what's next, and one large GO control. Show it as
   it would actually appear mid-session.
5. ONE PRINTED ARTEFACT. Practitioners hand out a paper sheet telling each
   player which bowls to use per section. Black on white, read at fifteen feet
   in a dim room by someone who does not read music.
6. A SHORT RATIONALE. A paragraph on the position you took and why — especially
   what you deliberately rejected.

────────────────────────────────────────
THE BAR

Someone should want this visible in their space. It should look like it belongs
next to a set of crystal bowls and a gong, not next to a laptop.

And it must never look like it is doing the healing. The humans are doing that.
```

## Notes for whoever sends this

**Send it to more than one place.** The point is divergence. Where two
independent attempts land on the same answer, that answer is probably determined
by the constraints and can stop being debated. Where they diverge, there's a
real decision to make.

**Don't include your own sketches or preferences.** Same reason as the build
handoff — you'd get your own idea back with better craft, which teaches nothing.

**The room paragraph is the most valuable input in the brief.** It's the part no
designer would guess and the part that will separate a real answer from a
generic wellness one. If anything gets cut for length, don't cut that.

**Judge the results on:** does it survive a dark warm-lit room; does it avoid
both spa and studio; would a practitioner be proud to have it visible; and does
it keep the human as the hero.
