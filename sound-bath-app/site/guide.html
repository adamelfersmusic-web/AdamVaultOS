Bed

Running a night

Everything that decides whether it works. Nothing else.

1You only need one device

Bed runs on one phone, tablet or laptop. That device plays the sound and you hold it, or set it down by the speaker. That's a complete session. Nothing else is required — not another phone, not a single one.

The other phones are the sheet. When people in the room join, Bed tells each of them which of their bowls to play right now:

2 6 (3 5 7) [1, 4]

Big — play freely. (Brackets) — sparingly. [Small] — rest, it fights the key. It's the thing that keeps eight people in one key without anyone counting.

But if you'd rather have no phones in that room, don't use them. Run Bed as one instrument and call the changes yourself, the way you always have. It does keep a group together, and it's genuinely easier — but it is a help, not a requirement, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

2Silence the phones

One buzz at minute thirty is the thing people remember about your night.

This matters more than anything else on this page, and Bed can't do it for you — no website is allowed to touch your phone's settings.

Think of it the way you think about tuning. No instrument on earth plays right without a step you do first, every time, before anyone is listening. This is that step. It takes fifteen seconds and it becomes part of how you set up, the same as putting the bowls out.

Everyone: Do Not Disturb

Before you lie down. It silences calls, texts and every app, and it keeps the connection alive so your sheet keeps updating.

Not airplane mode. Airplane mode cuts the connection, so the sheet freezes on whatever section was showing when you switched it on and never moves again.

If you want the phone fully off the network, that's fine — just don't expect it to follow along. Turn it off completely and play from the room instead.

⚠️ Especially the device making the sound

An incoming call doesn't interrupt the audio — it stops it. The floor drops out from under the whole room. Do Not Disturb on the leading device is not optional.

3Before the room arrives

Tell Bed what bowls are here

Ensemble → The group's bowls. Standard 7 (C D E F G A B) covers most sets. Everything Bed says is worked out from this, so it's worth thirty seconds.

And what they're tuned to

440 is standard; 432 is common in this world. Check rather than assume — a lot of sets sold as 432 aren't, and if this number is wrong every tone Bed plays will beat against your bowls all night and it will sound like the bowls' fault.

Number your bowls

1 is your lowest bowl, then up from there. The sheet is numbers, so the numbers have to be on the bowls — a small sticker on the outer wall, facing you, never on the rim.

Then play them 1 → 8 in order. It should sound like a scale going up.

If it doesn't, two are swapped — and everything Bed tells that person for the rest of the night will be wrong with no warning. Fifteen seconds, once, forever.

4Getting the room in

Share the sheet gives you a code and a QR. Tap the code to fill the screen, then put the phone or iPad down by the door and let people scan it on the way in. You can also send the link — it carries the code, so nobody types anything.

Send it out the day before if you can. The link is ready as soon as the session exists, it stays the same code, and people who tap it in the car park are already connected before they walk in. It's the smoothest version of this by a long way.

⚠️ Test it before the room fills

Scan your own code with a second phone. You'll see the count go up — "1 device is connected and holding" — and the phone will say You're in. That's the whole test, it takes twenty seconds, and it is the difference between finding a problem now and finding it in front of forty people.

The first join of the day can take up to a minute while the server wakes up. The phone says so while it waits. After that it's instant.

5During

BEGIN starts it. GO moves to the next section — you choose when, always. Bed never moves on its own, and there is no clock running the night.

Hold stays where you are for as long as you want. Nothing runs out.

Every phone in the room follows you within a second, including the section colour if someone's using a spare phone as a lamp.

If a phone loses signal it freezes on the last cue. That's deliberate — it becomes the printed handout it replaced, and the person keeps playing something correct. It catches up on its own when the signal comes back.

6The two-minute version

  • Bowls set in Ensemble, tuning checked
  • Numbers on the bowls — and they go up
  • Link sent out, or the code on a screen by the door
  • One phone scanned as a test — it says You're in
  • Do Not Disturb on every phone, starting with yours
  • BEGIN
