**The premise.** Bed is for sound bath practitioners who own crystal singing
bowls and can hold a room of 40–100 people, but can't compose, arrange, or run
a DAW. That gap is the only thing between them and leading a real session.
They spend $800 on one bowl without blinking. Not cheap, not clinical.

**The promise.** *You already have the love, the instruments, and the presence.
This is the first thing that lets you lead with them.*

**The line.** *One violin player becomes a symphony.*

**The closer** (ratified this session). *Say yes on Thursday.* — from the story:
Thursday a text lands, musicians you love are passing through, no event
planned; by Saturday there are sixty people on the floor because you spent
fifteen minutes on structure and the rest of the evening being present. The
promise isn't better sound baths — it's that you can say yes on Thursday.

**The posture.** The hero is always the person, never the software. The
product's job is to disappear. It must never look like it's doing the healing.

**The constraint behind every decision — the room.** Dark, washed in warm amber
from candlelight and tungsten. Screens read at ~4 feet at an oblique angle. The
person reading has a mallet in each hand. Warm colours vanish into that wash;
cool colours survive it. A bright or white screen there is genuinely
disrespectful and everyone present knows it.

**Non-negotiables — do not violate these:**
- Amber `#d9a544` means exactly one thing: *live and making sound.* Nothing
  else may ever wear it. This is enforced hard enough that the landing page's
  CTA is deliberately NOT amber — nothing on a marketing page is sounding.
- Never say "AI." In this market it signals inauthentic, extractive,
  machine-made spirituality. The wizard is "five questions."
- Never say "your audience can't tell the difference." True, and it reads as
  contempt for the craft to exactly the people whose endorsement matters most.
- Never make a claim about what a frequency does to a body. Chakra names and
  intents are labels only.
- Never white. No light mode, no "clean" deck variant. A page that only works
  on white contradicts the product.
- No waveforms, equalisers, or audio-reactive visuals, ever. The room is the
  visualisation.
- Cool colours carry information; the section arc is deliberately NOT the
  chakra rainbow.
- Serif = the person (titles, phrases, anything a practitioner wrote).
  Sans = the machine (controls, labels, data).

**What's in the kit:** wordmark + compact ring mark (a bowl from above, also
the GO ring), full palette with meanings including the ember palette the whole
app drops into once live, the type pairing with performance sizes chosen for
four feet rather than a desk, the nine-function section colour arc, motion
rules, and a documented set of judgment calls. Plus a landing page, a
two-minute film treatment as a real shot list (only three cuts of a screen in
the whole piece — that ratio is the philosophy), copy lines, the lead
screenshot choice, and a rationale of what was rejected.

**What I want from you:** you were there when this was just an idea. Read the
pass and tell me — where did it drift from the original vision, where did it
sharpen it, and what's missing that only you would notice?

Then help me answer the four blockers from the audit:
1. "Bed" is unsearchable — what's the public handle?
2. The film: documentary or staged with real practitioners?
3. Price posture — the copy promises "files you own, no account," which
   quietly forbids a subscription. Instrument-style one-time purchase?
4. What should the landing page's conversion event actually be?
