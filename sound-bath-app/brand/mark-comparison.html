The mark, resolved

two candidates · one system

What this page is. Bed ended up with two candidate marks, made independently, neither knowing about the other. This page puts them on the same ground, at the same sizes, in the same candlelit room, and scores them on tests rather than on taste — which is the brand's method everywhere else.

The ring was extracted from the working app. It is the favicon the app already shipped: a crystal bowl seen from above, and the GO ring that runs a session — one shape doing both jobs. It wears amber on the argument that it depicts a sounding bowl.

The stack came from an independent design pass on the written brief. Four layers — sub, drone, motif, texture — the strata of the bed seen from the side, heaviest line at the bottom where the sub lives. Its own rule: always stacked, always centred, never rotated, never boxed.

Nothing here is adopted. This is a decision document, pending Adam's call. If the stack is chosen it replaces the app favicon, the brand book's mark section, the landing page and assets/mark.svg; the canon then records that the mark came from the design pass and the wordmark came from the app.

The two marks, same ground

the ring — mine
the stack — theirs
The stack wins the mark. The didone wordmark wins the type. They are not in conflict — take both.

The stack is more product-true (four is a real number here), it lands the name — a cross-section of the sound and of a bed — and it puts the heaviest line where the sub lives, which is the actual differentiator. It's also ink rather than amber, so it obeys the amber rule without needing an argument. The ring needed one.

The one real risk at favicon size is the top two bars closing into a smudge. Widening the gaps slightly below 24px is a legitimate optical fix — the same instinct as the wordmark's optical re-centring.

The one thing to check before adopting

Flipped, this shape is the wifi/signal glyph, and four bars of varying length is adjacent to a level meter — which is on the brand's explicit anti-reference list (no waveforms, no faders, nothing that reads as music production software). It survives because it widens downward and the weight sits at the bottom: it reads as strata and mass, not as signal strength. Keep it stacked, centred, never rotated, never boxed — their rule, and it's the rule that keeps it out of the anti-reference.
