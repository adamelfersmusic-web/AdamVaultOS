Bed · bowl stickers · product sheet

One number.

Your bowl, your chakra, your note — all at once.

The app tells the room 2 6 (3 5 7) [1, 4]. Those numbers are worth nothing until they point at objects. These are the objects.

What the number means

3
  • Your bowl the third one up from your lowest
  • Your chakra Solar Plexus — courage and discipline
  • Your note the third of the key
The player learns none of it. They read a number, they hit that bowl, and they are right on all three at once. That identity is the design — it is why Bed never asks anyone for a key.

Where it goes

WhereOuter wall, upper third, facing the player. You should see it without moving your head.
NeverNot the rim. That is the striking and rubbing surface — it wears the sticker off, and it is the part that moves most.
Size18 mm. Readable at arm's length by candlelight; invisible from the floor.
Order1 is your lowest bowl. Work up from there.

The check that catches every mistake

Play them 1 → 8 in order. Does it go up?

If it does, they're on right. If it doesn't, two of them are swapped.

Fifteen seconds, no musical knowledge required, and it catches every possible mis-application. Stickering out of order breaks everything Bed says with no symptom at all — so the instrument gets read back before it's trusted, like everything else here.

Two versions

Plain is the one to make. Ring adds a cool position band — it encodes order, not a chakra-colour claim. Warm chakra colours are deliberately not used: the room kills them, and orange sits too close to amber, which in Bed means one thing only — live and making sound.

⚠️ This sheet is for C major sets

For a standard C major 7 or 8 — which is nearly every set in the room — the number on the bowl and the note it plays agree. Other tunings need their own sheet, and that decision isn't made yet. A sticker is permanent, so it is not the place to bake in an open question. See brand/numbering-and-tuning.md.

Print it

Cmd/Ctrl-P → A4, margins None, scale 100%. The circles come out at true 18 mm. Matte vinyl, not gloss — gloss catches candlelight and flashes. Test the adhesive on a bowl you don't love, and record before and after — these go on instruments people care about.
