Why this one and not a t-shirt

Sell the thing that makes the night better. Never the thing that advertises you.

A mask passes outright — it's part of the practice, not a souvenir of it. A shirt, a tote and a sticker all fail the same rule, and that distinction is the whole product line.

It is the practitioner's revenue, not ours. The only item on the whole list that puts money in their pocket the same night instead of taking some. That's the ecosystem posture in one object.

Quiet on purpose. A loud logo on sixty faces makes the room look sponsored, and a mask someone is faintly embarrassed by never leaves the drawer. A quiet one lives on a bedside table for years — which is worth more even measured purely as advertising.

Two things that would ruin it. Never lend them — a weighted pillow passed between strangers is a hygiene problem and, worse, an ick, in a market unusually attentive to exactly that. And never bundle one into a ticket: the moment it's included it stops being something someone chose and becomes something they were charged for.
