# The last screen

The single most valuable piece of marketing real estate this product will ever
have, and it already exists.

When a session ends, every follower's phone shows this — and nothing else:

> **The room is yours**
>
> *[the poem — every phrase the leader wrote, chained]*
>
> You were reading this from **Bed** — the instrument that holds you, so you can
> hold the room.
> *The person leading tonight built the structure; the room did the rest.*

Forty to a hundred people, just off the floor, coming back into themselves,
holding a phone. **It is the only sentence about Bed that a follower ever sees**,
and the last line hands the credit to the human. That's already right and it
should not be touched.

Adam, 2026-07-31: *"I wonder if we can make that sentence a link — drive them
right there — or not?"*

---

## First, a correction to the premise

**They're already there.** The follower's phone is *running Bed* — they opened
`/join` and entered a code. There is nowhere to drive them.

Which is better than it sounds, because it turns the question from *"how do we
send them somewhere"* into **"what is the right next step for someone who just
had the experience, on a device that already has everything."**

---

## The constraint that decides the design

At a sixty-person bath, most people are **attendees, not practitioners.** A call
to action addressed to all sixty converts the most defenceless moment of the
night into a funnel — and that is the one thing this brand cannot survive doing.

But some of that room *are* practitioners. `business-model.md`'s flywheel is
built on it: *nine practitioners experience the app from the inside every
session.*

> **So: invisible to the ninety who don't care. Unmissable to the ten who do.**

That has a real answer.

---

## Don't add a button. Make the sentence tappable.

**No visual change at all.** Same ink, same size, no chevron, no colour, nothing
that reads as an ask. The word **Bed** is simply live.

Anyone curious enough to tap it has **qualified themselves**, invisibly, at no
cost to the room. That's the whole mechanism, and it's better than any targeting
we could do, because the person did it.

---

## What's behind the tap — not the landing page

> ### "Keep tonight's session."

Not a signup. Not a sales page. **The session they just lay underneath, saved to
their phone.**

- It's a **gift, not an ask.** The night ends by giving them something.
- It's the sovereignty promise made literal — *a file you own, no account.*
- It costs **nothing to build**: the session is already on their device from
  sync. This is a save, not a download.
- And it lands them in a library **with one session in it** — the exact state in
  which *"make my own"* becomes the next thought. The paywall already sits at
  authorship, so the funnel is correctly placed with no further work.

### Never, at this moment

- **An account or an email capture.** Asking for an address at the end of a
  sound bath would be the most brand-damaging thing available to us.
- **A price.** Not here. Not ever here.
- **Anything that appears before the session has ended.**

---

## ⚠️ The leader's consent — this is not ours to give away

**That session is the leader's authored work**, and authored sessions are the
thing this business charges for. Handing one to sixty strangers automatically
gives our own customer's material away, in their own room, without asking.

**So it is a per-session choice by the leader**, in *Share the sheet*:

> ☐ **Let people keep this session**

**Default off.** Spread matters less than the fact that this is their work in
their room, and the posture is that the person is the hero.

When it's on, the card names them:

> *Emma left you tonight's session.*

Named, because featuring the person rather than the software is the rule
everywhere else too.

**When it's off**, the tap still does something worth doing — it opens the app's
own shipped sessions. A door, never a dead end, and never a pitch.

---

## The risk worth stating

Even a subtle tappable sentence, once practitioners know it's there, changes
their relationship to that moment. Some will feel that the software is quietly
recruiting their audience — and they wouldn't be wrong.

**That is the strongest argument for the toggle, and for default-off.** It's
their room. If a leader wants the room to find Bed, they can open the door
themselves. If they don't, the sentence stays what it is: a credit line that
hands the night back to the human who held it.

---

## The delayed reveal — considered, and I'd argue against it

2026-07-31, after the first working two-device session. Adam:

> *A lot of people would just click it after a bath and sign up. But it's a
> delicate moment — maybe it stays on that screen for 10 seconds before showing
> the Bed link.*

**The instinct is right and the mechanism backfires.**

### Motion after stillness is louder than presence

A thing that has always been there is **furniture** — the eye passes over it. A
thing that **appears** is an **event**, and motion is the most attention-grabbing
signal a screen has.

> A link that fades in after ten seconds doesn't read as *"here quietly if you
> want it."* It reads as **"…and now a word from our sponsor."**

The delay announces itself. **The mechanism that feels more respectful is the one
that intrudes more.**

### What protects the moment is layout, not timing

The card already does it: *The room is yours*, then the poem, then the Bed
sentence in `--ink4` — the faintest ink in the palette. **The moment comes first
because it is first and it is biggest.** Nothing has to hide for that to work.

And the moment is already protected by **state rather than a timer**: `.fafter`
only renders once the leader has ended the session. That's the honest version of
the ten seconds — the wait is real, not staged.

### ⚠️ "Sign up" is the word to guard against

Adam's phrasing gives away the risk in it. **Never an account at that moment.
Never an email. Never a price.**

Asking someone for an address ninety seconds after they came off the floor turns
the most defenceless moment of the night into a lead form. It is the single most
brand-damaging thing available to us, and it would be *effective*, which is why
it needs a rule rather than a preference.

> **Behind the tap is a gift, not an ask.**

*Keep tonight's session* — the thing they just lay underneath, on their phone.
Free to build, because sync already put it there. And it lands them in a library
with one session in it, which is the exact state where *make my own* becomes the
next thought. The paywall already sits at authorship, so the funnel is correctly
placed **without one word of persuasion.**

---

## How to choose — the decision, not the answer

Adam: *"This seems like an important product decision. I'm curious how we choose
it. If we do anything, the move is to do nothing and just keep it serving the
moment and let it spread — but it is the conversion moment."*

Four things decide it, in order. **None of them require taste**, which is the
point — this decision will come back in other clothes and it should resolve the
same way each time.

### 1 · The two errors are not the same size

| | If we're wrong | Cost | Recoverable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too little** | some practitioners who'd have converted don't | a few sign-ups | ✅ they find Bed later, the leader tells them, they search it |
| **Too much** | it reads as a pitch | **the moment is cheapened for everyone in the room, and the practitioner who invited them feels it** | ❌ you cannot un-cheapen a moment, and the person who noticed tells other practitioners |

**When the errors are asymmetric, optimise against the unrecoverable one.** That
alone points at *do less* before any argument about design.

### 2 · The test that actually discriminates

Not *"is it subtle enough"* — subtlety is a matter of opinion and everyone
believes their own ask is tasteful. Use this instead:

> ### Would it still be there if we were never going to sell anything?

| | |
|---|---|
| The poem | **yes** |
| *"The room is yours"* | **yes** |
| The credit to the leader | **yes** |
| *"Keep tonight's session"* | **yes** — they'd want the file whether or not we exist |
| A signup link | **no.** Obviously no. |

**The line isn't subtle-vs-loud. It's gift-vs-ask**, and that one can be applied
by anyone, tired, without judgement.

### 3 · ⚠️ "Do nothing" is not the neutral option, and it isn't free

Worth pushing back on the instinct, because it's the strongest counter-argument
and it changes the answer:

**That screen is already marketing.** *"You were reading this from Bed"* is a
credit line — it takes a moment of attention and gives nothing back. So the
choice was never between purity and commerce. It's between:

- a mention that **spends** attention and returns nothing, and
- a gift that spends the same attention and **returns something**.

> **Making it useful is more honest than leaving it as a credit line.**

Doing nothing feels like restraint. It's actually the version that takes without
giving — it's just too quiet for anyone to object.

### 4 · Then don't decide it from principle at all — measure it

The first night in Ohio settles this better than any argument. **Run it exactly
as it is.** Afterwards, ask two people:

> *"Did you see the last screen? What did it say?"*

- **Nobody remembers it** → the screen converts nobody and the delicacy is
  theoretical. Add the gift; there's nothing to protect.
- **People mention it warmly** → the moment lands. Now you know what you'd be
  risking, and you can weigh it with real information instead of instinct.

**Measure the moment before monetising it.** It costs one question and it
replaces the entire debate.

---

## The rule, for every future version of this question

> ## At the moment of most feeling, only give. Never ask.
> ### If there's nothing to give, put nothing.

Simple enough to apply in a hurry, and it resolves the whole class in advance —
email capture, ratings, share buttons, *tell a friend*, "rate your experience",
a newsletter, a discount. **Every one of them is an ask at the moment of most
feeling, and every one of them is answered by that sentence.**

It also explains why *keep tonight's session* survives while everything else
fails: **it's the only candidate that is a gift.**
