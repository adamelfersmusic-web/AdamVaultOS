01 · Wordmark

Three letters, a lot of air

BED, set in a didone, all caps, tracked .28em. The tracking hangs a phantom space after the final letter, so the app optically re-centres with padding-left: .28em — keep that trick wherever the wordmark is centred. Never bold, never tinted, never on white. Ink (#e8e4f0) on the ground; ember ink (#d9c7a4) when the surface is live.

The tag — holding space — rides 6px beneath, 11.5px, tracked .3em, uppercase, in the interface voice (system sans, --ink3). It is optional everywhere except the app masthead and the wordmark lockup file.

The compact mark

Four layers: sub, drone, motif, texture — the strata of the bed seen from the side, heaviest line at the bottom where the sub lives. It is a cross-section of the sound, and of a bed. Four is a real number in this product, not a decoration, so the mark encodes the architecture rather than gesturing at a mood.

The mark is ink, never amber — and never animates.

Ink, because the rule can't hold an exception at its most visible point. Amber means live and making sound. An earlier mark — a thin amber ring, a bowl seen from above — needed an argument to wear it (it depicts a sounding bowl). The argument was sound and it was still a carve-out, in a system whose whole power is that amber has no exceptions. This kit refuses an amber call-to-action on a marketing page because nothing there is sounding; that refusal is indefensible while the logo wears amber on a technicality. The stack removes amber from the identity system altogether.

Static, because bars that light up one at a time are a level meter — which is on the anti-reference list. The shape survives that adjacency only by widening downward with the mass at the bottom, so it reads as strata rather than signal strength — and that is a defence of a still mark. It matters most in the two places the mark has no context to protect it: a browser tab, where four ascending bars read as wifi in 200ms, and a follower phone, where an animated mark means a roomful of small meters glowing in candlelight. The band row inside the app earns its amber by being one row, on one screen, read deliberately. A favicon earns nothing.

Always stacked, centred, never rotated, never boxed. Rotation is what turns it into the wifi glyph.

Construction: four bars in a 32 grid, widths 6.8 / 11.6 / 16.8 / 22 and weights 1.2 / 1.45 / 1.75 / 2.2 — both width and weight increase downward, so the sub reads as the heaviest thing. Ink #e8e4f0 on #0a0910. One master. The gaps are tuned for 16px and hold at 96px, so there is no small-size variant to maintain and no chance of the two drifting apart. The dark plate travels with the mark; on a light surface, bring the plate. Files: assets/mark.svg, assets/wordmark.svg.

The ring was demoted, not deleted

The previous mark — a thin amber ring — is still in the product, doing the job it was always actually doing: it is the GO control. Its problem was never the shape. It was a UI element doing double duty as a logo, which is precisely why it needed special pleading to wear amber. Demoted, both systems get simpler: amber leaves the identity entirely, and the ring keeps its amber for the only honest reason — it is live and making sound.

02 · Palette

Cool carries information. Warm disappears.

The rooms Bed lives in are washed in candle and tungsten amber. Warm colours on a screen vanish into that wash; cool colours survive it. So everything that must be read is cool, and everything that must recede is dark or warm-and-dim. The ground is near-black with a violet cast — never pure black, which reads as a hole in the room, and never white, which is a flashlight.

Amber #d9a544 means one thing: this is live and making sound. Nothing else may ever wear it.

In the app it marks the sounding state and the single control that begins sound — GO, the ring, the position line, the filled band dots, the slider thumb only while your finger is changing the live sound. One amber control per screen, at most. Outside the app — pages, decks, cards — amber may appear only inside a depiction of the product sounding. Not on CTAs, not on highlights, not on headings. Amber is candle-coloured on purpose: sound and flame are the two live things in Bed's rooms, and they share a colour.

The ember palette — what live looks like

Once a session begins, the whole interface drops to ember: warm, dim ink on near-black, because a bright screen in that room is disrespectful and everyone present knows it. Chrome joins the candlelight; only information keeps its colour — the section hues stay cool, amber stays amber. Nothing needs to know: the palette swaps under everything via body.live, over .8 seconds.

03 · Type

The serif is the person. The sans is the machine.

The pairing is semantic, not decorative. Everything a practitioner wrote or means — session titles, section names, phrases, the wizard's questions, the poem — is set in the didone. Everything the software does — controls, labels, numbers, data — is set in the system sans. When you can't decide which face a thing takes, ask who is speaking.

The app takes no webfonts: the didone resolves to Didot on Apple, Bodoni MT on Windows, Georgia as the floor. Where an artefact must embed a font, the canonical didone is Playfair Display — open licence, already in the stack.

04 · The section colour arc

Nine functions, one dusk

Sections are coded by function, not by chakra — deliberately not the rainbow. All nine hues are cool and muted so the strip reads as one dusk: no hue shouts, and the arc becomes a session's fingerprint rather than a chart. It appears identically at three sizes — the library card strip, the wizard summary, and the thin arc above the perform screen.

The four bands

The lanes borrow from the arc rather than adding hues: Deep #5d6aa8, Bed #8b7ab8, Voice #a68cc0 (the drone's hue, lifted), Air #4d8b96. In performance the bands speak as SUB · DRONE · MOTIF · TEXTURE — filled amber when sounding, hollow when silent — because live, the only question is what is sounding, and that question is amber's alone to answer.

05 · Motion

Nothing moves that isn't time or sound

The complete list of things that animate continuously in Bed: the position line crawling the arc (time), the GO ring filling (time), the hold button breathing (waiting on a human), the word migrating pulsing (the key literally in transit), and the band dots crossfading (sound entering or leaving). That's all. Everything else changes state by fading — 120–180ms, colour and border only.

  • Never faster than the sound. The audio establishes over 35–45 seconds; the UI must not promise snappier physics than the room has. The one spatial move in the app — a drawer rising 12px — takes 180ms and stops.
  • The big transition is slow. Going live warms the whole screen over .8s — candlelight coming up, not a mode switch.
  • Continuous motion is linear or breathing. The clock crawls linearly; pulses are sinusoidal. No spring curves, no bounces, no overshoot — ever.
  • Sound is never visualised as data. No waveforms, no equalisers, no audio-reactive particles — not in the app, not in marketing. The band row is filled or hollow, not a meter. The product's whole posture is that the room is the visualisation.

The app's own microcopy is part of the voice and may be quoted as marketing: "the app plays only what humans physically can't — the floor, the drone, the air", "a roster, not a mixer", "the room owns time", "holding — the plan waits." Write new copy in that register: short, warm, certain, technically honest, second person, and always about the person.

Never
  • "Your audience can't tell the difference." True, and it reads as contempt for the craft to exactly the people whose endorsement matters most.
  • "AI." In this market the word signals inauthentic, extractive, machine-made spirituality. The wizard is "five questions." The engine is "the engine."
  • Anything about what a frequency does to a body. Chakra names and intents are labels only. No health claims, ever.
  • Bright by default. A white page is the visual version of all three.
07 · Judgment calls

Where the app hadn't settled, and what I decided

CallDecision & why
The amber rule vs. primary buttonsThe app paints "New session / Begin / GO" amber. I ruled this consistent: amber marks sound or the single control that brings it closer — at most one per screen. Evidence the app means it: the tone slider's thumb turns amber only while a finger is actively changing the live sound.
Wizard dots & focus outlinesThe wizard's progress dots and the keyboard focus ring are amber, and neither is sound. That's drift, not intent. Proposed app fix (not silently applied): both go violet — the shaping-hand colour. Flagged here per the "propose, don't replace" rule.
Two vocabularies for the four bandsDesign says Deep / Bed / Voice / Air (the metaphor you build with); performance says SUB / DRONE / MOTIF / TEXTURE (what a musician parses mid-strike). I kept both on purpose: "Bed" as a lane label on the live screen would collide with the wordmark, and the acoustic names are self-explaining at a glance. Design keeps the poetry; performance keeps the physics.
The mark's colourThe favicon existed without a rule. I formalised it: the ring is a sounding bowl, so it wears amber legitimately — and therefore the mark is never used as decoration at large scale next to unrelated amber-free content. It's a signature, not a pattern.
No amber on marketing chromeNothing on a landing page is making sound, so the landing CTA is not amber — the only amber on the page lives inside the product frame, which depicts a live session. The rule taken seriously enough to cost us the conventional glowing button.
Marketing stays in the "before" palettePages and decks use the violet-black waiting palette, not ember — ember is earned by a session actually running. A brochure pretending to be live would be a small lie.
Canonical embedded didoneThe app trusts the OS. When an artefact must ship a font, use Playfair Display — it's in the app's own stack and openly licensed. Didot remains the ideal; nothing may substitute a non-didone.
