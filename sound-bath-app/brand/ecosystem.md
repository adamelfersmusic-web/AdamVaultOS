# The ecosystem — one system, and the network around it

From Adam, 2026-07-31, in conversation. His vision dump plus the pushback and
sequencing worked out with it. Nothing here is committed; it is the map.

---

## The moat is integration, not bundling

> *One app, one light rig, one speaker, one team.*

Right — and the mechanism is precise, which is what makes it defensible:
**everything subscribes to one cue stream.** The follower sheets, the lights,
the projection are not integrated products, they are *the same state message
with different renderers*. `section:change`, `key:migrate` and `fade:start` have
ridden that bus since day one.

A competitor can assemble a bundle in a quarter. They cannot retrofit one cue
stream into four products built separately — that decision has to be made
before any of them exist, and it was.

## The practitioner network — directory, not marketplace

**The idea:** most practitioners don't know improvisers who can do this work,
and most improvisers can't. Adam, as a musician, can tell which players cross
over — jazz or classical backgrounds, people who fit a sound bath room. *"It's
a very particular person."*

**That judgment is the asset.** Not the booking.

### Why not a marketplace

- Two-sided cold start, and liquidity has to be solved **per city**
- Payments, no-shows, trust and safety — a whole company
- **Disintermediation is the classic killer**: after one gig they text each
  other directly. Taking a cut of live work is historically very hard to hold.

### Why a directory + credential is stronger

- **Valuable at ten names.** No liquidity required.
- **Can't be routed around** — the value is the vetting, not the transaction.
  Being *on the list* is the product.
- **It's already half-built.** Ensemble is a roster: the app knows instruments,
  sections and keys. *"Three Bed players near you"* is the app doing it, not a
  second product.
- **It is the growth loop with names.** BOOK: nine practitioners experience the
  app from the inside every session, and some fraction want to lead their own.
  A directory of players who have already done it makes that visible — every
  name on it is a practitioner-in-waiting.

### Revenue, honestly

Not a cut of gigs. Candidates, in order of how well they hold:

1. **Players pay to be listed and verified** — a guild, not a broker.
2. **It's free and it's the growth engine** — probably correct early. The
   directory's job is to make sessions happen, and sessions sell leader licences.
3. **Featuring is the marketing** — a vetted list and "who we post about" are
   the same list. It costs nothing and it's the reason to be on it.

## Packs

Already specified in BOOK: curator identities with named packs, each a coherent
set of *sections* rather than a track. Packs are presets — kilobytes, no CDN,
they migrate key with the room, and wavetables let a pack have genuine sonic
identity while staying tiny. *Splice ships gigabytes; this ships JSON.*

Cold-start fix, from BOOK: the house makes the first three or four in-house
curator identities, then **local legends** write packs and become affiliates.
That is the same set of people as the directory — the network and the
marketplace are one population, approached twice.

---

## Events — sequencing matters

**Show up at other people's events before throwing your own.** Equipping a
local legend's night is cheap, produces the footage, and builds the same story.
Throwing your own is operationally heavy and you would be paying to learn what
a partnership teaches for free.

**Artist collaborations (East Forest and similar) come after proof, not before.**
The film is the proof. A named artist joins something that visibly already
works; nobody lends their name to a deck.

The honest order:

1. Real sessions with real practitioners → footage, sheets, quotes
2. The film
3. Equip and support other people's nights
4. A named collaboration
5. Bed's own events, if they still look necessary by then

---

## What this all shares

Every branch above resolves to the same asset: **a small number of people who
lead rooms well, who are visible to each other.** The directory, the pack
curators, the affiliates, the local legends and the film's cast are one
population.

That is worth saying out loud, because it means the sequencing is simpler than
it looks: **find and serve fifteen practitioners properly, and every one of
these becomes available at once.**
