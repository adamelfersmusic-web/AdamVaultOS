# Eye masks

2026-07-31. Adam: *"DUDE — blindfolds that say Bed, sold at events."*

This is a better hardware product than the lights, and the lights were already
the good one. Reasoning below, including the two things that would ruin it.

---

## It passes the rule `hardware-strategy.md` was built on

> **Sell the thing that makes the night better. Never the thing that advertises
> you.**

That rule killed speakers and approved lights. An eye mask passes it outright:
**people at sound baths already use them.** Light blocking and a little weight on
the orbital bone measurably deepens the drop — it's part of the practice, not
a souvenir of it. Nobody has to be persuaded it's useful.

**A t-shirt, a tote, a sticker all fail the same rule.** Those are merch. This
isn't, and the distinction is the whole thing.

---

## Why it's better than the lights

| | Lights | Masks |
|---|---|---|
| Sells to | practitioners — **hundreds** | attendees — **tens of thousands** |
| Cost to make | electronics, firmware, batteries | fabric, flax, lavender |
| Support burden | real (it can fail mid-session) | none |
| Shipping | box, weight, customs | flat, light, anywhere |
| Warranty | yes | no |

**The market is two orders of magnitude bigger and the product is a hundred
times simpler.** Lights are still worth doing; this is worth doing first.

---

## The part that makes it strategy rather than merch

**It is the practitioner's revenue, not ours.**

Sixty people in a room. Twenty buy a mask at $28. **The practitioner clears
about $320 on the night** on something they didn't have to make, carry a
warranty on, or learn anything to sell.

| | |
|---|---|
| Cost to produce | ~$5–8 |
| Wholesale to practitioners | ~$12 |
| They sell at | $25–30 |

We make a real margin. **They make a bigger one.** That is the entire ecosystem
posture in one object: *make the practitioner more successful and the business
follows.* It is also the only thing on the whole list that puts money in their
pocket the same night rather than costing them some.

---

## The object

**Tone on tone. The mark barely there.**

Ink on violet-black — the mark felt more than seen, the way it is everywhere
else. A loud logo on sixty faces is the exact opposite of *the hero is always
the person*, and it would make the room look sponsored.

**This costs us the impressions and buys the brand.** Correct trade, and not a
close call: a mask people are slightly embarrassed by never leaves the drawer,
and a mask that's quietly beautiful lives on a bedside table for years. **The
second one is worth more even measured purely as advertising.**

*(And the name does the work no design could: a **bed** is what you wear one in.
Same accident as everything else about that word — see `taglines.md`.)*

---

## ⚠️ Two things that would ruin it

**1 · Never lend them. Only sell them.**

A weighted eye pillow passed between strangers across events is a hygiene
problem and, worse, an *ick* — in a market unusually attentive to exactly that.
Each person keeps their own. This is also why it's a revenue product rather
than equipment: selling is the hygienic model *and* the profitable one.

**2 · Never bundle it into a ticket.**

The moment it's included, it stops being a thing someone chose and becomes a
thing they were charged for. Sold at a table, in the light, before or after —
never during, and never automatically.

---

## What to test, and it's cheap

**Order 50. Give them to two practitioners you know. See how many sell at a
real event.**

No manufacturing commitment, no branding decisions locked, no inventory risk
worth naming. If they sell out, this is a product line. If they don't, you're
out a few hundred dollars and you learned it before designing anything.

Run it at the same events as the five affiliate deals in
[`affiliates.md`](affiliates.md) — same rooms, same people, two answers for one
trip.
