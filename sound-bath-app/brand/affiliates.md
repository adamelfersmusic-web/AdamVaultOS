# Affiliates

2026-07-31. Adam: *"honestly for marketing we should get some affiliates and let
it rip — give our affiliates a kickback or something, some benefit."*

Right instinct, and this market is unusually well-suited to it. But **what the
kickback is** decides whether this builds the thing or corrodes it, so that's
what this note is about.

---

## Why affiliates fit here better than almost anywhere

- **The buyer is unreachable by advertising and completely reachable by
  trust.** There is no keyword to buy. There *is* a teacher whose recommendation
  ends the conversation.
- **The scene already runs on referral.** Nobody finds their first sound bath
  through a search engine.
- **You only pay for outcomes.** A small market can't fund paid acquisition;
  it can absolutely fund a share of revenue that already happened.
- **The product already ships the demo.** A shared session file *is* the pitch —
  see [`sessions-travel.md`](sessions-travel.md). The affiliate doesn't need
  assets; they need a link to the session they already made.

---

## ⚠️ The trap, stated first

`ecosystem.md` and `business-model.md` both end at the same place:
**Couchsurfing's asset was trust and it damaged itself by monetising that trust
directly.** Bed's asset is the same one.

**A commission on a personal recommendation converts a teacher into a
salesperson, and everyone in this scene can smell that.** The first time a
student suspects their teacher got paid to say it, the recommendation is worth
nothing — and so is the next one.

So the design constraint is sharp:

> **Pay for the thing they'd have done anyway. Never for the sentence they say
> about you.**

---

## The version that works: pay for teaching, not for talking

The people who would move real numbers here are **teacher-trainers** — the
practitioners who run trainings and certifications, with ten to thirty students
a year who are *all* standing at the exact threshold the product is built for.

Their students already pay for a course. Bed inside that course isn't a
recommendation; it's curriculum. **Nobody is suspicious of a required text.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | teacher-trainers, retreat leaders, studio owners running series |
| **What they do** | teach with it — students each run their own copy |
| **What they get** | see below, and it is deliberately not just cash |

---

## What the kickback should be

**A revenue share alone is the weakest version.** It's the most legible, and
it's the one that turns them into salespeople. Stack it third, not first.

### 1 · Seats for their students — free, for the course

The single strongest incentive, and it costs nothing marginal. A trainer whose
twenty students all have working Bed accounts during the training looks
generous, teaches better, and hands us twenty practitioners at the exact moment
they're deciding what kind of facilitator to be. **Conversion happens on its
own** when the course ends and the seat lapses into the free tier.

### 2 · Their session in the monthly

`the-monthly.md` is a curated session a month, **authored by a named
practitioner**. That slot is the most valuable currency in this whole plan and
it isn't money: it is *distribution and credit*. For a teacher building a
practice, being the author of the month is worth more than a cheque, and it
feeds the thing the subscription is for.

### 3 · A share of what their students pay — modest, and *only* after the seat

**20% for the first year, on subscriptions that started as a course seat.** Not
lifetime, not stacked, not on hardware.

Deliberately unaggressive, and the reason matters: **the money must never be
large enough to be the reason.** If a trainer would choose Bed over a better
tool for their students because of the commission, the program has already
failed, and the students will eventually work that out.

### Never
- **A public affiliate link program.** Open sign-up turns the scene's trust into
  an arbitrage opportunity for people with no practice and a mailing list.
- **Commission on the network or the introductions.** Charge for the instrument;
  never for the connection. That rule is already load-bearing in
  `business-model.md`.
- **Discount codes with the affiliate's name on them.** They make the payment
  visible at the exact moment the recommendation lands, which is the worst
  possible place for it.

## How to start — cheap, and it doubles as the price test

Do not build a program. **Do five deals by hand.**

1. Five trainers you or your network actually know.
2. Free seats for their next cohort. Nothing else, no contract, no dashboard.
3. Watch one number: **how many students still have a session six weeks after
   the course ends.**

That number is the whole business. If it's high, the program is real and worth
building software for. If it's low, no commission rate would have saved it — and
you found out for the cost of some free seats.

It also runs `business-model.md`'s test #3 (*does a follower become an author?*)
against a warm, motivated cohort instead of strangers.

---

## The line

**Affiliates here are teachers, not marketers. Pay them in students, credit, and
last of all in money** — in that order, because that's the order that keeps the
recommendation worth having.
