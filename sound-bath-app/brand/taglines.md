# Taglines — a running list

Open file. Nothing here is settled except what's under **Fixed**. Add lines as
they arrive, dated and attributed; don't delete losing candidates — a line that
fails today often supplies a word to the one that wins.

Started 2026-07-31, when Adam said *"not sure but somewhere is a good tagline,
might not have found it yet."* That's the right posture. Most of these are
close, and close is not it.

## The five tests

Derived from canon, not invented here. A line that fails 1 or 2 is dead
regardless of how good it sounds.

1. **Is the person the hero?** The posture is absolute. A line about what the
   software does fails, however elegantly it does it.
2. **Does it survive the gear frame?** The business model says always present
   Bed next to a bowl, never next to an app — *"nobody pays four figures for
   software they open on a phone; they do pay it for a system."* **"Instrument"
   is doing commercial work, not poetic work.**
3. **Is it differentiated in this market?** Every practitioner's bio in this
   scene already says *holding space*. Warm, true, and invisible.
4. **Does it fit the treatment?** The tag under the wordmark is 11.5px,
   `.3em` uppercase — roughly **14 characters** before it wraps on a phone.
   `HOLDING SPACE` is 13. Anything longer needs a different treatment, not a
   smaller font.
5. **Does it collide with the word "Bed"?** See the hazard below.

---

## ⚠️ The hazard: BED + ROOMS

**`BED` directly above or beside `ROOMS` reads as *bedrooms*.** It's the
strongest candidate direction we have and it has a trap in it.

- `BED / ROOMS YOU LEAD` — **dead.** The two words are adjacent and the eye
  fuses them.
- `Bed — for rooms you lead` — a dash and a preposition between them. Survives,
  but it's thin ice.
- `Bed — an instrument for rooms you lead` — **safe.** Three words of spacer;
  the collision never forms.

The direction is good enough to be worth protecting. Any line using "rooms"
needs something between it and the wordmark.

| an instrument you lead | **Grammar misfires.** You lead *rooms*; you *play* an instrument. As written it says you lead the instrument, which is backwards — the instrument follows you. |

### 2026-07-31 · Adam, second pass — **hold vs guide**

| Line | Read |
|---|---|
| **guide the room** | **The leading tag candidate.** 14 characters — drops into the tag treatment with no redesign. Second person. No BED+ROOMS collision. Native to this avatar in a way "hold" isn't quite: *guided* meditation is a fixed phrase in this scene. |

#### Why "guide" and not "hold" — Adam was right, and for a better reason than active/passive

The first read of this filed **hold** as the winner, on the grounds that *hold*
is already the spine of the whole system: the `HOLD` control, *"holding — the
plan waits,"* *"Bed **holds** the structure of a sound bath,"* and the market's
own *holding space*.

**That's backwards. Being the spine makes it unavailable, not obligatory.**

If Bed holds the structure and the tagline tells *you* to hold the room, the two
holdings compete, and the one thing this brand can't afford to blur is who is
doing what. The clean division is the product itself:

> ### Bed holds. You guide.

Bed holds the things that must not wobble — the floor, the arc, the keys. You
guide the room: the bowls, the moment, the human decisions. **The tagline should
name your half, never the half the software does.**

Two corrections to the record while we're here:

- **The A8 objection was misapplied.** *Bed diagnoses, it never prescribes* is a
  rule about the software's behaviour toward the practitioner. "Guide the room"
  has *you* as the subject. There is no conflict; the rule was never about the
  person.
- **"More active" was the right instinct with the wrong label.** The gain isn't
  energy, it's **agency** — "guide" credits the practitioner with authorship of
  the night. Someone who designed a fifty-minute arc is not containing a room,
  they're taking people somewhere.

> **`guide the room` is now the leading tag candidate, `an instrument for rooms
> you lead` the leading descriptor, and `Bed holds. You guide.` may be better
> than both** — it's the only line that states the division of labour, which is
> the whole argument for the product in four words.

*Room* is emerging as the brand's central noun across all three. That's a good
noun to settle on: physical, where the night actually happens, and the thing the
practitioner is genuinely responsible for.

## The avatar, stated properly — and what it does to the brief

Adam, 2026-07-31:

> *The avatar is the person that already loves this, they're just not able to
> lead it on their own. This is about them **stepping into it**.*

This is the sharpest statement of the buyer we have, and it **changes what a
tagline has to do.**

Every candidate above describes a competent professional's ongoing practice —
*hold the room*, *guide the room*, *rooms you lead*. But the buyer is standing
at a threshold, not mid-practice. The love is already there. The bowls are
already bought. **What's missing is the first yes.**

That is exactly what the promise already says, and it's why the promise is
better than everything in this file: *"You already have the love, the
instruments, and the presence. This is the first thing that lets you lead with
them."* The word doing the work is **first**.

Three consequences:

1. **It settles guide vs hold, again and independently.** An imperative —
   *guide the room* — is an invitation across a threshold. *Hold the room*
   describes a steady state you're already in. For someone who hasn't led one
   yet, only one of those is addressed to them.
2. **The film's end card and the product line may want to be the same line.**
   *Say yes on Thursday* is the only thing in the entire kit that is explicitly
   about stepping in. It has never been considered as the tagline. It should be.
3. **"Instrument" gets more load-bearing, not less.** You don't step into a
   practice by buying software. You step into it by picking up an instrument.

## Where this is heading

Two slots, two registers — and the mistake would be forcing one line into both.

- **The tag** (under the wordmark): must be short. `HOLDING SPACE` still holds
  the slot because nothing shorter has beaten it, not because it's good.
- **The descriptor** (the sentence you say when someone asks what it is; the
  `<title>`, the meta description, the App Store line): **"an instrument for
  rooms you lead"** is better than what's there now, on tests 1 and 3.

**Nothing has been changed in the product.** Per Adam's standing instruction —
*"if you find a genuinely better line while working, propose it in a note
rather than silently replacing these"* — this is the note.
