# Bed — Marketing Kit

Companion to the brand kit (`brand/index.html`) and the landing page
(`site/index.html`). Everything here honours the amber rule: **amber
(`#d9a544`) appears only where something is live and making sound.**

---

## 1 · The film — "Thursday"

Two minutes. Three cuts of a screen in the entire piece — that ratio is the
philosophy: the technical layer disappears. Everything else is a room, and
people, and light.

**Production notes, before the shot list**

- **Sound is diegetic only.** The film's score is the session itself, recorded
  in the room. No library music, no sweetening. If the room can't carry a film,
  the product doesn't work.
- **Grade:** tungsten and candle. Warm amber wash throughout; skin and brass
  and wood. The app's live screen is ember — dim warm ink on near-black — so it
  sits inside the grade without dimming in post. It is the only screen prop in
  the film that never strobes the colourist.
- **Cast real practitioners.** The insider shot (8) only lands if the hands are
  real. Bowls handled the way owners handle them.
- **The pronoun is load-bearing.** The closing line is **"We made it happen."**
  "We" is the people in the room, never the app. Do not let an edit soften it
  to "it" or "Bed."
- **No captions except the title card.** Shot 8 especially: no caption.

## 2 · Copy — for a page or a card

The settled lines, in the order you'd reach for them:

1. *You already have the love, the instruments, and the presence. This is the
   first thing that lets you lead with them.*
2. *One violin player becomes a symphony.*
3. **Say yes on Thursday.** — the short form; use the long form where it needs
   its setup: *The promise isn't better sound baths. It's that you can say yes
   on Thursday.*
4. *The sound design was never the art. Leading the room is.*
5. *It plays only what humans physically can't — the floor, the drone, the
   air.* (lifted verbatim from the app's own ensemble screen; extraction, not
   invention)

**Provenance note:** #3's short form was proposed in v1 under the "propose in
a note, don't silently replace" rule, and ratified by Adam the same day. It is
now settled: it closes the landing page and the film's end card. The long form
remains canonical wherever the story hasn't been told yet.

## 3 · The one screenshot to lead with

**The perform screen, live** — ember palette, mid-session in *Full Moon in
Leo*: "Heart Chakra / What's left", the bowl line, 4:12 remaining, the amber
ring just past half, and the band row reading **SUB ● DRONE ● MOTIF ○
TEXTURE ●** — motif hollow because there's a flute in the room.

Why this one:

- It is the product at its truest: almost nothing on screen, everything
  implied to be in the room. A screenshot that is mostly darkness is the
  promise kept.
- The hollow MOTIF dot is the posture in one pixel: the software visibly
  yielding to a human. No caption needed for practitioners; a one-line caption
  ("there's a flute in the room, so the app's motif rests") does it for
  everyone else.
- Amber appears exactly where sound is — the rule, demonstrated.

**Rejected:** the design timeline. It's the denser, prettier, more
"impressive" screenshot — and that's exactly the problem: it makes the
software look like the author. The timeline can appear second, never first.

## 4 · Rationale — the position, and what was turned down

**The position.** Bed's brand is the discipline of staying out of the light.
The interface is set dressing for a room lit by candles; every artefact —
page, film, deck, card — is judged by the same test as the app: *could this be
glanced at from four feet, at an angle, in candlelight, without disrespecting
the room?* Cool colours carry information because the rooms are washed warm.
Amber is reserved for sound because sound and flame are the two live things in
that room. The serif is the person; the sans is the machine. Nothing moves
that isn't time or sound.

**Rejected, deliberately:**

- **White.** No light mode, no white marketing variant, no "clean" deck
  template. A page that only works on white contradicts the product.
- **The chakra rainbow.** The one place colour was obvious — seven chakras,
  seven colours — the app already refused; sections are coded by *function*,
  all cool, all muted. The kit keeps refusing. This is the single largest
  extracted decision in the system.
- **Waveforms, equalisers, audio-reactive anything.** The brand never
  visualises sound as data. The engine has no pulse; the visuals get none
  either. The band row is filled or hollow — a state, not a meter.
- **An amber CTA.** Nothing on a marketing page is making sound, so the
  landing page's buttons are ink-on-panel with a violet hover. The rule cost
  us the conventional glowing button, and paying that cost is what makes the
  rule real.
- **"AI," anywhere.** The wizard is "five questions." The engine is "the
  engine." (The word also never appears in the app — that's extraction too.)
- **Frequency claims.** Chakra names and intents are labels. Nothing anywhere
  says what a sound does to a body.
- **"Your audience can't tell the difference."** True and forbidden — it
  reads as contempt for the craft to exactly the people whose endorsement
  matters most.
- **Screen-forward advertising.** Three cuts of a screen in a two-minute film,
  and the lead screenshot is mostly darkness. The hero is always the person.
