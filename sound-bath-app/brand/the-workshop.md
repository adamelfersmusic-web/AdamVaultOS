> projector) · ask questions, live support, group learning · **then you LEAD a

**This is the strongest thing on the whole list, and I think it should happen
before the affiliate program, before Lumen, before the masks.** Reasoning below,
including the shape change that makes the last step actually possible.

---

## Why it beats everything else queued

### 1 · It's the avatar's entire arc, compressed into one day

The buyer is *someone who already loves this and hasn't led one alone yet.*
Every note in this folder circles the same word: **threshold.**

> **They arrive never having led one. They leave having led one.**

That is not a marketing framing of the day. It's the literal schedule. And it's
the promise, executed rather than claimed: *"This is the first thing that lets
you lead with them."*

### 2 · It runs the test the business has been waiting on — live, with witnesses

`business-model.md` test #1: *can a first-timer reach a runnable 50-minute
structure in fifteen minutes?* Called a hard spec, because if the answer is no
the positioning collapses.

**A workshop answers it in one afternoon**, in a room, with eight people trying
at once — and if it fails, you see *exactly where* they got stuck, which no
survey and no analytics would ever tell you.

**But it does *not* answer test #3, and I said it did.** Workshop attendees paid
to be in the room — they're pre-qualified and pre-motivated, which makes them the
strongest cohort that will ever exist. That's perfect for testing the
fifteen-minute claim and it tells you **nothing** about whether a *cold* follower
converts. See the three conversions in `business-model.md`: a workshop measures
**C**, adoption by people who already lead. The flywheel is **B**, and only a real
session with real follower devices can measure it.

### 3 · The projector is a demo nobody could buy

Watching someone else build an arc, and hearing the room react to it, is how the
model gets learned. It's also the only setting where **held sections become a
group activity** — hold it, everyone hears the balance, someone says *the motif
is too loud under that drone*, you fix it in front of them.

**v1.5 turns out to be a workshop feature.** It wasn't designed as one.

### 4 · It makes more money than the software it teaches

| | |
|---|---|
| 12 seats at $250–400 | **$3,000–4,800, one day** |
| 12 annual subscriptions | $1,788, one year |

**The workshop out-earns a year of the software for everyone in the room.** Which
means it isn't marketing overhead — it's the near-term business, and the
subscription is the residue it leaves behind.

### 5 · It's the affiliate program, run by you first

`affiliates.md` says the channel is teacher-trainers, because their students are
all at the threshold and Bed is *curriculum* rather than a recommendation.

**This is you being that teacher-trainer.** Which is the right order: you cannot
hand a program to five trainers until you have run the day once yourself and
know what actually happens in hour three.

## ⚠️ The one thing that would ruin it

**If the day is "how to use Bed," it's a webinar and nobody pays for it.**

> The day is **"lead your first sound bath."** Bed is *how*, never *what*.

Same rule as everywhere else: the person is the hero, the bowl is the companion,
Bed is the thing that gets them over. Concretely:

- **Open with bowls, not screens.** Hands on metal in the first hour. If the day
  opens with software, the room correctly reads it as a sales seminar and never
  fully comes back.
- **No pitch at the end.** Not one. They will have authored a session they want
  to keep, and keeping it is the paid tier — the paywall lands on its own, at the
  moment of highest intent, which is exactly where the model was designed to put
  it. **A pitch would convert a threshold into a transaction and it is the only
  thing that could sour the day.**

## The shape: everyone leads, and the room becomes their ensemble

Adam's refinement, and it's better than leading a section of a shared bath:

> *Everyone syncs to **your** Bed session. **You** lead. The other participants
> become **your ensemble.***

**That is the real thing, not a rehearsal of it.** You are not contributing a
piece to somebody else's arc — you are holding a room, and the room is eight
people with bowls in their hands who are following *your* sheet.

Then the next person leads, and you become part of theirs.

### Why this is the strongest form of the day

- **It's the threshold, crossed in public.** Not "I built a session." *I led one,
  and people played under me.*
- **Everyone experiences both ends.** You feel what it is to lead, and — this is
  the part no marketing could buy — **you feel what it is to be led well.** Every
  person in that room now knows exactly what their own students will feel.
- **The follower view stops being a feature.** Eight phones showing your bowls
  for your section, in your key. Nobody has to be told what sync is for; they
  just spent two hours inside it.
- **It fits the clock.** Ten to fifteen minutes each, eight people, is about two
  hours — a real afternoon, not a seven-hour endurance test.
- And it delivers the line literally: **one violin player becomes a symphony**,
  except there really are eight of them, and they're following you.

### The shape of the day

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Bowls. Technique, hands on metal, no screens. |
| **2** | The model — arc, sections, keys, the floor. Taught with bowls, not with the app. |
| **3** | Bed. Build **your** session: name, phrase, chakra, the lanes. |
| **4** | The projector. One at a time, on the wall — held, heard, and fixed in front of everyone. |
| **5** | **You lead. They follow.** Each person in turn, everyone else on the sheet, bowls in hand. |
| **6** | **Names and numbers.** Before anyone leaves. |

Step 4 is the one that will be tempting to cut for time and the one that must
not be. It is where the learning happens, and it's the reason the room is a room.

---

## Step 6 is not a nice-to-have. It's the network.

> *Encourage them to connect with each other at the end, and create ensembles.*

**This is `ecosystem.md`'s hardest problem, solved by a room instead of by
software.** "Three Bed players near you" needs a directory, a map, accounts,
trust and a critical mass. Eight people who just played under each other need a
piece of paper.

They already have the two things no platform can manufacture: **they've heard
each other play, and they know who was good.** That is the entire value of a
practitioner network, and it exists by dinnertime.

Make it explicit and slightly formal — *"before you go, find the two people
you'd play with"* — because a room full of people who just cried together will
absolutely mean to and absolutely forget.

## The first night — and it is not the workshop

2026-07-31. Adam: *"I know 10 of these people in Ohio right now."*

**That is the single most valuable asset in this entire folder**, and it is worth
being exact about what it is.

Those ten are **conversion C** — practitioners who already lead and have never
followed a Bed session. Real revenue, not the flywheel. But:

> **One evening converts them into the flywheel's only input.**

You lead. They're your ensemble, holding the sheet in their own kits. Now ten
people who already run rooms have experienced Bed **from the inside**, which is
the one thing conversion **B** requires and the one thing money cannot buy.

Then the number, and there is only one:

> ### Six weeks later, how many of the ten built and kept a session of their own?

That is `business-model.md` test #3, run for the price of one evening, with
people who will come because you asked. **Nobody with a spreadsheet gets to run
that experiment.**

The workshop is the packaged version. This is the raw one, and it comes first:

### What it does and does not prove

- ✅ **The flywheel.** Whether experiencing Bed produces authors. The one thing
  nothing else can test.
- ✅ **The fifteen minutes**, for anyone who tries.
- ✅ **The relay, sync and follower kits**, under real load, in a real room, in
  the dark.
- ❌ **The market.** `business-model.md` is blunt about it: *"ten warm sales
  validate your relationships, not a market."* **Buyer eleven is still the test.**

That last line is not a caveat to skip past. **Ten friends will say yes to
almost anything**, and the number that matters is not whether they turn up —
it's whether they are still using it when you aren't in the room.

## What to do first, and it's small

**Run it once. Eight people you know. One day. Charge something real** — free
attendance produces free attention, and you need to know whether a person will
put money down before they've seen it.

Two numbers to watch, and only two:

1. **How long the first person takes to reach a runnable section.** If it's

   it in public.

   `affiliates.md` cares about. It's the only number that says whether any of
